About The Sunday Table

A small, independent recipe publication for people who cook at home.

What this site is

The Sunday Table is an online recipe publication. It collects everyday recipes, from breakfasts and packable lunches to weeknight dinners and a handful of reliable desserts, and explains them clearly enough that you can cook them successfully the first time.

Why it exists

Many recipes online are hard to use. Ingredient amounts are vague, timings are optimistic, and the one detail that makes the dish work is buried or missing. We want each page here to be the one you keep coming back to: measurements in both US and metric where practical, numbered steps, realistic times, and notes on what to do when you do not have an ingredient.

What we publish

  • Familiar dishes that home cooks actually make, written with care rather than novelty for its own sake.
  • Recipes using ingredients found in ordinary supermarkets.
  • Substitutions, variations, storage and reheating guidance for each recipe.
  • Practical technique in our kitchen guide.

How recipes are developed and selected

Recipes are chosen because they are useful: they solve a real mealtime need, work with standard equipment, and can be explained clearly. We do not claim that every recipe has been cooked and tested. Cooking times and results can vary by kitchen, ingredients and equipment.

Editorial philosophy

  • Clarity first. Every step should be something you can act on.
  • Honesty about time. Total time includes the real work, and chill or rise times are listed separately.
  • No sensational claims. We describe food plainly. We do not make health, weight-loss or medical claims, and nutrition figures are labelled as estimates.
  • Content over clutter. The recipe is the point of the page. If advertising is ever added, it will be clearly separated from recipe content and will never be disguised as navigation or buttons.
  • Corrections welcome. If something does not work, please tell us. We will review and update the recipe.
