From the kitchen guide

Six habits that make every recipe work better

Small, repeatable things that matter more than any special equipment. The full kitchen guide goes deeper.

  1. Read the whole recipe first

    Before you turn on the heat, read every step. Rests, chill times and "reserve the pasta water" are easy to miss halfway through.

  2. Prep before you cook

    Chop, measure and line up ingredients first. Fast methods like stir-frying leave no time to mince garlic mid-recipe.

  3. Salt in stages

    Season pasta water, then the sauce, then taste at the end. Small amounts at each stage taste better than one big pinch.

  4. Dry what you want to brown

    Pat chicken, fish and chickpeas dry. Surface moisture has to evaporate before browning can begin.

  5. Give food room

    Crowded pans steam instead of sear. Cook in batches or use two pans.

  6. Trust a thermometer

    An inexpensive instant-read thermometer takes the guesswork out of meat, fish and even custards.
