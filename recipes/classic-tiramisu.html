Classic Tiramisu

Espresso-soaked ladyfingers layered with mascarpone cream and cocoa, set overnight.

Jump to recipe

About this dish

Tiramisu is assembled, not baked, and it gets better with time. A night in the refrigerator lets the ladyfingers soften to a cake-like texture and the flavours settle.

This version gently cooks the yolks with sugar over simmering water (a zabaglione method) so the cream is rich and stable, without raw yolks.

Method

  1. Whisk yolks, sugar and marsala in a heatproof bowl set over barely simmering water. Whisk constantly 6 to 8 minutes until thick, pale and 160°F (71°C). Cool 10 minutes.

  2. Beat the mascarpone briefly until smooth, then fold in the cooled yolk mixture.

  3. In another bowl, whip the cream to soft peaks and fold it into the mascarpone mixture.

  4. Pour the coffee into a shallow dish. Dip each ladyfinger for 1 to 2 seconds per side and lay in a single layer in an 8x8-inch dish.

  5. Spread half the cream over the ladyfingers. Repeat with another layer of dipped ladyfingers and the remaining cream.

  6. Cover and refrigerate at least 6 hours, ideally overnight. Dust with cocoa through a sieve just before serving.

Cook's notes

  • Dip quickly. Soaked ladyfingers make a soggy tiramisu.
  • Keep mascarpone cold and do not overbeat it, or it can turn grainy.
  • Dust cocoa at the last minute so it stays velvety, not damp.

Substitutions

  • Alcohol-free: leave it out and add 1 teaspoon vanilla.
  • Decaf espresso works well.
  • Sponge cake strips can replace ladyfingers.

Variations

  • Fold grated dark chocolate between the layers.
  • Make individual portions in glasses.
  • Use strawberries and orange juice instead of coffee for a summer version.

Storage

Keeps covered in the refrigerator up to 3 days. Can be frozen up to 1 month; thaw overnight in the refrigerator.

Reheating

Serve chilled. Not suitable for reheating.

Questions

Does tiramisu contain caffeine?

Yes, from the espresso. Use decaf if serving to anyone avoiding caffeine.

Can I skip cooking the yolks?

Traditional recipes use raw yolks, but gently cooking them is recommended, especially when serving young children, older adults or anyone pregnant.
