Garlic Butter Chicken

Seared chicken thighs basted with garlic, thyme and butter, with a pan sauce made in the same skillet.

Jump to recipe

About this dish

Basting is an old restaurant technique that works beautifully at home. Once the chicken is browned, you tilt the pan and spoon foaming garlic butter over it again and again, so it seasons the meat while it finishes cooking.

Boneless thighs are forgiving and cook in about fifteen minutes. Serve with something to catch the sauce: mashed potatoes, rice, or a good loaf.

Method

  1. Pat the chicken dry and season both sides with salt and pepper.

  2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the chicken smooth side down and cook undisturbed 5 to 6 minutes until well browned.

  3. Flip, reduce heat to medium, and add butter, garlic and thyme.

  4. As the butter foams, tilt the pan and spoon it over the chicken continuously for 3 to 4 minutes, until the chicken reaches 175°F (80°C).

  5. Move the chicken to a plate. Add stock and lemon juice to the pan and simmer 2 minutes, scraping up the browned bits.

  6. Return the chicken and any juices, spoon sauce over, and scatter with parsley.

Cook's notes

  • Do not move the chicken while it browns; it releases from the pan when ready.
  • Thighs are at their best around 175°F (80°C), higher than breasts, because the connective tissue softens.
  • If the butter starts to burn, lower the heat and add a splash of stock.

Substitutions

  • Use chicken breasts, pounded to even thickness, cooked to 165°F (74°C).
  • Rosemary or sage can replace thyme.
  • Dry white wine can replace half the stock.

Variations

  • Add sliced mushrooms to the pan after removing the chicken.
  • Stir 2 tablespoons cream into the sauce for a richer finish.
  • Add a spoonful of Dijon to the pan sauce.

Storage

Refrigerate chicken and sauce together for up to 3 days.

Reheating

Warm covered in a skillet over medium-low heat with a splash of stock, about 6 to 8 minutes, or microwave in short bursts.

Questions

Can I use bone-in thighs?

Yes. Brown skin side down, then finish in a 400°F (200°C) oven for 20 to 25 minutes before basting on the stovetop.
