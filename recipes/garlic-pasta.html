15-Minute Garlic Pasta

Spaghetti with gently toasted garlic, olive oil, chili and parsley, in the spirit of aglio e olio.

Jump to recipe

About this dish

This is close to the Italian classic spaghetti aglio e olio, with a little parmesan added. It uses almost nothing and depends entirely on technique.

The garlic should turn pale gold, never brown, and the starchy pasta water is what turns olive oil into a silky coating rather than a slick.

Method

  1. Cook the spaghetti in well-salted boiling water until 1 minute short of al dente. Reserve 1 1/2 cups pasta water.

  2. While the pasta cooks, put the oil and garlic in a large cold skillet. Set over medium-low heat and cook 4 to 6 minutes, swirling, until the garlic is pale golden.

  3. Add the pepper flakes and immediately ladle in 1/2 cup pasta water to stop the cooking.

  4. Add the drained pasta and toss vigorously over medium heat for 1 to 2 minutes, adding more pasta water until glossy.

  5. Off the heat, add parsley, parmesan and lemon zest. Taste for salt and serve.

Cook's notes

  • Starting garlic in cold oil gives you more control and prevents scorching.
  • Brown garlic tastes bitter; if it goes too far, start again. It is only garlic and oil.
  • Toss energetically to emulsify the sauce.

Substitutions

  • Linguine or bucatini both work.
  • Pecorino can replace parmesan.
  • Gluten-free spaghetti works; save extra pasta water.

Variations

  • Add sautéed shrimp.
  • Toss in a handful of arugula at the end.
  • Top with toasted breadcrumbs for crunch.

Storage

Best eaten immediately. Leftovers keep 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating

Warm in a skillet with a splash of water and a drizzle of olive oil.

Questions

Why is my sauce oily instead of silky?

There was not enough pasta water or not enough tossing. Add more water and toss hard over the heat until the oil and water combine.
