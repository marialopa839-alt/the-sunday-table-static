Honey Garlic Salmon

Pan-seared salmon finished with a sticky honey, soy and garlic glaze in about twenty minutes.

Jump to recipe

About this dish

Salmon rewards a hot pan and a light touch. Sear it hard on one side for colour, then turn the heat down and let a quick glaze reduce around it.

The glaze balances honey with soy sauce and a splash of rice vinegar, so it reads as savoury and bright rather than dessert-sweet. It is equally good spooned over rice or roasted vegetables.

Method

  1. Pat the salmon very dry and season the flesh with salt and pepper.

  2. Whisk together honey, soy sauce, rice vinegar, garlic and ginger in a small bowl.

  3. Heat the oil in a large nonstick or well-seasoned skillet over medium-high heat. Lay the salmon in skin side down and press gently for 10 seconds.

  4. Cook 5 to 6 minutes until the skin is crisp and the flesh is opaque about two-thirds of the way up.

  5. Flip the fillets, lower heat to medium-low, and pour in the glaze. Simmer 2 to 3 minutes, spooning it over the fish, until it thickens and the salmon reaches 125 to 130°F (52 to 54°C) for medium.

  6. Serve with the glaze, green onions and sesame seeds.

Cook's notes

  • Lower the heat before the glaze goes in. Honey burns quickly.
  • Salmon keeps cooking after it leaves the pan, so pull it slightly early.
  • Center-cut fillets cook more evenly than thin tail pieces.

Substitutions

  • Maple syrup can replace honey.
  • Use tamari for a gluten-free version.
  • Lime juice can replace rice vinegar.

Variations

  • Add a teaspoon of sriracha or chili crisp to the glaze.
  • Bake instead: brush with glaze and roast at 400°F (200°C) for 12 to 14 minutes.
  • Use the same glaze for chicken thighs.

Storage

Refrigerate up to 2 days in an airtight container.

Reheating

Warm gently at 275°F (135°C) for 10 to 15 minutes, or serve chilled flaked over salad.

Questions

How do I know when salmon is done?

It should flake easily at the thickest part and look just slightly translucent in the centre. An instant-read thermometer reading 125 to 130°F (52 to 54°C) gives a moist medium.

Can I use frozen salmon?

Yes. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and dry well before cooking.
