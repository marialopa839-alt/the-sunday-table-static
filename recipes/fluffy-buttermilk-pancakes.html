Fluffy Buttermilk Pancakes

Tall, tender pancakes with a gentle tang, made from a lumpy batter that is allowed to rest for ten minutes.

Jump to recipe

About this dish

Good pancakes are mostly about restraint. The batter should be stirred until the flour just disappears and then left alone, lumps and all. Overmixing develops gluten and gives you flat, chewy rounds instead of soft, risen ones.

Buttermilk does two jobs here: its acidity reacts with the baking soda for lift, and it adds a mild sourness that keeps the stack from tasting one-note sweet. A ten-minute rest lets the bubbles form before the batter ever touches the pan.

To serve

Method

  1. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda and salt.

  2. In a second bowl, whisk the buttermilk, eggs, melted butter and vanilla until smooth.

  3. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ones and fold with a spatula until no dry streaks remain. The batter should still be lumpy. Let it rest for 10 minutes.

  4. Heat a heavy skillet or griddle over medium-low heat for 3 to 4 minutes. Brush lightly with butter.

  5. Pour 1/4 cup of batter per pancake. Cook until bubbles appear across the surface and the edges look set, about 2 to 3 minutes.

  6. Flip once and cook 1 to 2 minutes more, until golden and springy in the centre. Keep finished pancakes warm on a rack in a 200°F (95°C) oven while you cook the rest.

Cook's notes

  • Your first pancake is a thermometer. If it browns before bubbles appear, lower the heat.
  • Do not press pancakes with the spatula after flipping; it squeezes out the air you rested the batter to build.
  • Wipe excess butter from the pan between batches so the surfaces brown evenly instead of frying.

Substitutions

  • No buttermilk: stir 2 tablespoons lemon juice into 2 cups milk and let stand 5 minutes.
  • Swap up to half the all-purpose flour for whole-wheat flour for a nuttier pancake.
  • Use neutral oil instead of butter in the batter if needed.

Variations

  • Blueberry: scatter berries onto each pancake right after pouring rather than stirring them into the batter.
  • Lemon-poppy seed: add the zest of one lemon and 1 tablespoon poppy seeds to the dry mix.
  • Brown butter: brown the butter before adding it for a toasty, nutty flavour.

Storage

Cool completely, then refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days or freeze between sheets of parchment for up to 2 months.

Reheating

Toast from chilled or frozen in a toaster, or warm on a baking sheet at 350°F (175°C) for 8 to 10 minutes.

Questions

Why are my pancakes flat?

The usual causes are overmixing, old baking powder or soda, or a pan that is too cool so the batter spreads before it sets.

Can I make the batter the night before?

It is best fresh. The leaveners start working as soon as they meet the buttermilk, so overnight batter rises less. You can mix the dry ingredients ahead.
